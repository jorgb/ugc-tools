## 2026-09-20 - Volume: converted samples are too quiet on the MPC (your plan)

### The problem
- OUTERSPACE hi-hat (SP404 pad A15) has pad volume 16 and every step at
  volume 90 (the SP404's default step volume, not chosen). Converted, that is
  pad level 0.7079 * 16 / 127 = 0.0892 and note velocity 90 / 127 = 0.7087.
  It sounds far too quiet on the MPC Sample, and most other samples are quieter
  than on the SP404 too.
- Measured from the SMP files (peak / RMS in dBFS, pad volume):
  kick A13 -0.1 / -14.5 (80), snare A14 0.0 / -22.6 (36), hi-hat A15
  -10.2 / -35.8 (16). A plain linear 16/127 gain would put the hi-hat about
  24 dB below the kick, so the SP404 cannot be doing that (it sounds fine there).
- Unknowns, none of them can be read from the files we have:
  1. How the SP404 turns pad volume (0-127) into gain.
  2. How it turns step velocity into gain, and whether pad volume also applies
     to notes played by a pattern (PTN.txt lists "Recorded volume of pad" as
     never tested).
  3. On the MPC: is the volume float 0.7079 0 dB (DESIGN.md 5.4 says so) or -3 dB
     (the MPC format notes say so)? And what curve does note velocity follow
     (every pad has velocitySensitivity 1.0)?
- Code to change afterwards: mapping.instrument_volume() and
  mapping.note_velocity(), the vol row in DESIGN.md 5.4.

### Part A - MPC, about 10 minutes, no recording needed
1. Open the converted OUTERSPACE on the MPC Sample
   (testing/MPC/projects/PROJECT_01.xpj works). The SP404 pad rows are flipped:
   A01 is MPC pad 13, A13 is pad 1, A14 is pad 2, A15 is pad 3.
2. In the Pad Mixer write down the dB shown for pads 13, 1, 2 and 3. The floats
   we wrote are 0.7079, 0.446, 0.2007 and 0.0892.
3. On four other pads set the level by hand to 0 dB, -6 dB, -12 dB and -24 dB,
   save as testing/MPC/projects/VOLUME_LAW.xpj. I read the floats back with
   convert/xpj_to_json.py, which gives the exact float <-> dB table.
Result: tells whether 0.7079 is 0 dB and what dB our other values really are.

### Part B - SP404, the important one, about an hour
Idea: RESAMPLE records what the SP404 really outputs. Exporting the project
gives SMP files I can measure with the parser, so no ears or audio interface.

Setup (keep it identical for every recording)
- One loud, short, clean sample on a source pad (the A13 kick, peak -0.1 dBFS).
- Pad: one shot, attack 0, release 0, hold 100, pitch 0, BPM sync off, bus FX
  dry. No effects on, master and bank volume left at their defaults (127).
- Resample the main output, nothing else.

B1 - pad volume law (pad hit by hand)
- Turn FIXED VELOCITY on (plays at 127, so velocity is out of the picture).
- Set the pad volume (Pitch/Speed page) to 127, 96, 64, 32 and 16. For each,
  hit the pad once and resample onto a new pad. 5 recordings.

B2 - velocity law (pattern)
- Pad volume 127, FIXED VELOCITY off. Make a pattern with one step and set that
  step's volume in the microscope to 127, 90, 64 and 32. Play the pattern and
  resample each. 4 recordings.

B3 - your real case (pattern plus pad volume)
- Pad volume 16, step volume 90 in a pattern, resample. 1 recording. Compare
  with B2's 90 (pad volume 127): this shows whether pad volume applies to
  sequenced notes at all, and how.

Bookkeeping
- Write which resampled pad is which setting in
  testing/SP404mk2/volume-law/README.txt (for example "A01 = vol 127, fixed").
- Export the project to testing/SP404mk2/volume-law/ (PADCONF.BIN, SMPL/, PTN/).

### Part C - MPC audio, only if Part A does not settle it
Measure the MPC's own curves with its resample: a pad at 0 dB and notes at
velocity 127, 90, 64 and 32, then the pad level at 0, -6, -12 and -24 dB with
velocity 127. The WAVs land in the project's _[ProjectData] folder. Save as
testing/MPC/projects/VOLUME_LAW_AUDIO.xpj.

### Part D - what I do with the results
1. Measure peak and RMS of every recording relative to the 127/127 reference,
   and fit the SP404 pad volume law, its velocity law, and how pad volume and
   step volume combine in a pattern.
2. Change mapping.instrument_volume() and mapping.note_velocity() (and the pad's
   velocity sensitivity if needed) so the MPC lands on the same level. Update
   DESIGN.md 5.4 and the tests with the measured numbers.
3. Re-convert OUTERSPACE and compare kick / snare / hi-hat balance against the
   SP404 by resampling both.
4. Also look at FIXED_VELOCITY pads (A09, A10, B08 in OUTERSPACE): they play at
   127 on the SP404, but we do not apply it, so they come out too quiet too.
